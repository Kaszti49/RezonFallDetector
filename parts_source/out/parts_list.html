Signal chain

SM-24 geophone → ADC 15 Click → SPI → ESP32-S3
ESP32-S3 → I²S → PCM5102A → PAM8403 → Speaker
ESP32-S3 → UART → SIM868 (Waveshare) → GSM call → our phone
Plus a WS2812B status LED (green / amber / red) and an “I’m OK” button. One 18650 cell feeds the SIM868 directly and a 5 V boost for everything else.

SIM868 setup

  • Pull the Pico H off the SIM868 board: it is not used. The ESP32-S3 drives the board through 5 M-F jumpers into its Pico sockets: pin 1 RXD ← GPIO17, pin 2 TXD → GPIO18, pin 3 GND, pin 19 PWRKEY ← GPIO16, pin 36 3V3 ← ESP32 3V3.
  • Power it straight from the 18650 (3.4–4.4 V) into the board’s PH2.0 battery socket, its power slide switch on the battery side. Never 5 V; header pins 39 (VSYS) and 40 (VBUS) stay empty.
  • Bursts up to 2 A while transmitting: 1000 µF at the take-off on the perfboard, short 0.5 mm² leads.
  • Logic: the board has its own 3.3 V ↔ 2.8 V level shifter (fed from pin 36), so no divider any more.
  • Power-on: GPIO16 HIGH for 1.5 s (an NPN pulls PWRKEY low), then wait for RDY. NET LED: fast blink = searching, about every 3 s = registered.
  • GSM antenna on the “GSM” u.FL socket before the first power-on. GNSS and BT antennas are not needed.
  • SIM868 is 2G only: Hungary still runs 2G (Telekom’s 2G now runs on Yettel’s network); confirm coverage at the venue.
  • Turn the SIM PIN off in a phone first; test AT → AT+CPIN? → AT+CREG? and a call before the show.
